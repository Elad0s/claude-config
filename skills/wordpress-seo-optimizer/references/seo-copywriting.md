# Writing the titles & meta descriptions

The mechanics (previous files) are the easy part; the copy is where the value is. Default to proposing a full set in a table for the user to approve before applying — meta tags are public-facing.

## Decide the brand voice once
Pick a tone with the user up front (it sets everything): polished-professional, warm-with-a-wink, or bold-punchy. Mine the site for voice cues (taglines, "about" copy, any mascot/brand device) so the copy sounds like them, not generic. A quick way to get sign-off is to show the same example rewritten in 2–3 tones and let them choose.

## Titles (the clickable `<title>` / SEO title)
- **Under ~60 characters** so Google doesn't truncate.
- Pattern that works for local service businesses: `{Service or Place} {Keyword} | {Brand}` — e.g. `Lexington Chimney Sweep & Repair | Top Dog Chimney`, `Chimney Repointing in Greater Boston | Top Dog Chimney`.
- Lead with the **primary keyword + geo**; brand at the end. **Unique per page.** No keyword stuffing.

## Meta descriptions (~150 chars, the snippet under the title)
- ~150 characters (≈120–158). One clear value prop / soft CTA.
- **Unique per page — this is the big one.** The most common failure on location/service-area sites is the *same templated sentence with only the city swapped*. Google treats near-duplicate descriptions as low quality and often won't show them. **Vary each page by a real local angle**: historic homes, coastal/salt air, long winters, older housing stock, "our home town", county landmarks, the specific towns inside a county, etc.
- Follow the user's constraints (e.g. **no phone number** if they ask — phones eat characters and the click-to-call is on-page anyway).
- Write in the chosen brand voice; a light, human hook beats a feature list.

## Examples (warm-with-a-wink voice, chimney service business)

**Location page**
Input: a "Lexington, MA" service-area page (historic colonials)
Title: `Lexington Chimney Sweep & Repair | Top Dog Chimney`
Desc: `Your chimney works hard — so do we. Top Dog Chimney keeps Lexington homes warm, safe, and clean, because your family (and your dog) breathe it too.`

**County / region hub**
Input: a "Plymouth County" taxonomy archive (coastal, towns: Hingham/Plymouth/Duxbury)
Title: `Plymouth County Chimney Sweep & Repair | Top Dog Chimney`
Desc: `Coastal weather is hard on chimneys. Top Dog Chimney keeps Plymouth County homes — Hingham, Plymouth, Duxbury — swept, sealed, and safe year-round.`

**Service page**
Input: a "Chimney Repointing" service
Title: `Chimney Repointing in Greater Boston | Top Dog Chimney`
Desc: `Cracked mortar? We've got it. Top Dog repoints with color-matched mortar that locks out water and keeps your chimney standing strong for years.`

## Coverage checklist for a local-service site
Don't stop at the homepage. A typical job covers: every **service** page, every **location / service-area** page, every **region/county taxonomy** archive, the core pages (Home/About/Contact/Blog), and any **portfolio/project** items. Use the API inventory from Step 1 of the SKILL so nothing is missed, and keep a running count of what's done vs. what's verified.
